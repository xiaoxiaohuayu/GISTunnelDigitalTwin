# Visual direction

The user delegated visual choices while requesting a science-fiction character and a change away from green-grey. The existing scene and controls remain product authority; the surrounding shell changes.

Seven grounded references considered were motorway wayfinding, civil-protection publications, aerospace atlas typography, construction material catalogues, long-exposure night-road photography, optics publications and modular infrastructure signage. The assigned third direction is an aerospace-atlas palette and typography applied to familiar web controls, not imitation instrumentation.

Use blue-black (#070d20) and deep indigo (#111b3b) grounds, electric blue (#6cbaff) selection and a small violet (#aa96ff) accent. Reserve green for open lanes and active equipment; amber and red indicate simulated caution and incident states. Main text is #edf4ff and secondary text #abbfdc. Keep the scene dominant, with bottom scene navigation, a collapsible simulation tray on the left and mutually exclusive equipment/control and device inspection panels on the right. No fabricated health percentages or charts. Device readings and alarm scenarios must be explicitly labelled as local simulations.

The signature interaction is the four-stage Earth-to-portal arrival; controls support skipping and replay. Respect reduced motion. Retain ordinary buttons, fieldsets, selects and expandable lists. Do not restore the removed descriptive overlay or the separate Three.js mode.

Product text is Chinese. Prefer installed Chinese system type for controls and a local bundled font if one already exists; the visual identity comes from hierarchy, consistent spacing and the spatial entrance, not ornamental English labels. Keep danger controls explicitly labelled as local demonstrations.
